# Design-partner outreach templates

Status: **draft; personalize from a real workflow hypothesis**

These messages ask for learning and observation. Do not claim customers,
production readiness, generic ROI, compliance, or capabilities outside the
current product boundary.

## Warm introduction request

Subject: Introduction to a data/analytics leader with a painful weekly report

> I'm building AMOS, an internally deployed analyst system for recurring
> business reviews. It is meant to let an AI draft the analysis and artifacts
> while keeping calculations, evidence, permissions, and final approval outside
> the model. We're interviewing leaders who own a weekly executive, finance,
> revenue, risk, or operations report—not selling a broad AI-governance project.
> Could you introduce me to someone who owns both the workflow and the budget?
> I'd like 30 minutes to understand the current process and, only if useful, a
> second session to observe one real run.

## Direct problem-interview request

Subject: How does your team produce and verify [specific recurring review]?

> Hi [name]—I'm researching one narrow problem: recurring business reviews
> where an analyst gathers warehouse data, applies approved metrics, explains
> changes, builds charts/slides, and gets the result approved. AMOS is a local
> prototype for producing that package with claim-level evidence and human
> review, but we have not validated the customer workflow yet. Would you spend
> 30 minutes walking me through the last [review/report] your team completed?
> This first call is discovery; no data access or demo is needed.

## Follow-up for workflow observation

Subject: Observe one [workflow name] run

> Thank you for explaining [specific pain/failure]. The useful next step would
> be to observe one recent run from request through source/metric selection,
> analysis, artifact preparation, review, delivery, and later correction. Values
> and sensitive fields can be redacted, and we will not request credentials or
> retain artifacts without approval. In 60 minutes, could [daily user], [data
> owner], and [reviewer] walk through the current process and baseline time?

## Paid-pilot transition

Subject: Fixed-scope shadow pilot for [workflow]

> Based on the observed workflow, I think there may be a bounded test: one
> read-only source, [metrics], no more than three query shapes, one report and
> slide template, one reviewer, and one export destination. AMOS would run in
> shadow mode for four weekly cycles beside the existing process. Before any
> access, we would agree on the data/security boundary and freeze success
> thresholds for number accuracy, unsupported claims, permission safety,
> analyst/reviewer time, and support burden. If this scope matches a current
> priority and paid budget, can we schedule the buyer, data owner, reviewer, and
> security stakeholder to decide scope, fee, and a commercial decision date?

## Short objection prompts

Use these to learn, not overcome the objection reflexively:

- Existing platform: “Which part already handles claim-level calculation
  support, reviewer correction, and later source-change impact? May I observe
  the current workflow before deciding AMOS adds value?”
- No budget: “Which role owns the cost of the current workflow or its errors,
  and when is that budget decided?”
- No access: “Is the blocker policy, security review, source ownership, or lack
  of urgency? What evidence would change it?”
- Too early: “What minimum deployment, connector, or security evidence would be
  required before a paid shadow test?”
- Wants broad strategy: “Which single recurring deliverable would make the
  strategy concrete enough to measure?”
- Wants free proof of concept: “What paid decision would the proof unlock, who
  owns it, and what would make the result a no-go?”

## Evidence to record after every message or call

- account and role (in the approved private system);
- exact workflow hypothesis and why the person is relevant;
- response, objection, and direct customer language;
- whether a qualified interview or workflow observation was booked;
- named next action, owner, and date; and
- consent and location for any retained notes or artifacts.
